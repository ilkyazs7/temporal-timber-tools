nothing is finished

manifesto

the dream of a responsive, information-rich architecture is old. sutherland gave digital objects their own properties [1]; the eameses treated information itself as something to be designed [2]; alexander argued design should begin by structuring information, not form [3]; lewitt showed that a rule set can be the work, its outcomes left to unfold [4]; pask, price, and negroponte imagined buildings and machines that learn from the people who use them [5]. less explored is what happens when a material carries a record of how it has aged and been cared for, and that record informs how we design with it.

there are no fixed entities. a material is not a static specification but a process; it weathers, degrades, and is repaired, its future emerging, as ingold [6] and barad [7] remind us, through ongoing intra-action between matter, environment, and the people who tend it. yet we still design as if materials were inert and buildings finished.

time is a material. to design with it is to trade static specification for temporal alignment: to track a material's lifespan and condition, and let that record inform every decision that follows. we call for interactive information tools, material passports that align with maintenance records, and temporal models embedded in bim that turn the biography of matter into foresight. not to predict the future, but to give designers and decision-makers the knowledge to navigate an uncertain one: to know what a material is, how it has been cared for, and what it might yet become.

nothing is finished.
